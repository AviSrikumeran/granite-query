# Next method: projected student-gradient alignment

Design and separate collector code prepared 2026-11-03. No gradients have been collected and no paid work has been launched for this method. The running random/skill-diversity arms, inputs, optimization settings and results remain unchanged. The implementation update below explicitly changes the proposed projection and future source cohort.

The next research question is concrete: **under the same supervised-token training budget, do examples whose updates align with Granite's target reasoning gradients improve accuracy more than random or skill-based selection?** This makes the student model's own response to each example part of selection. It is more direct than lexical similarity or classifying a question into a weak skill.

## Primary evidence

[LESS, ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/xia24c.html) uses LoRA warmup, projected gradients, Adam-aware candidate directions and target-example similarity. Its first-order loss proxy is neither a Shapley value nor a causal measurement of an example's contribution. Warmup and additional checkpoints mattered in the authors' ablations. Importantly, their GSM8K appendix reports random 5%=17.0, LESS 5%=21.0, full data=30.5: selected small data did **not** beat full in-domain data in that experiment. Their four-checkpoint/8192-dimension defaults are a reference, not a promise for Granite. [Paper sections 4, 6 and D.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04333v3#S4).

The verified [official LESS code](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/LESS/tree/8abf9628b9a814ac3045445eebc8ba3c908fdc78) is pinned at `8abf9628b9a814ac3045445eebc8ba3c908fdc78`. Its [warmup configuration](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/LESS/blob/8abf9628b9a814ac3045445eebc8ba3c908fdc78/less/scripts/train/base_training_args.sh) uses four epochs, LoRA r=128/alpha=512, q/k/v/o projections, length 2048, LR 2e-5, 3% warmup, AdamW and zero weight decay. Our smaller r=8 q/v configuration and shorter warmup below are deliberate budget adaptations, so the experiment must be named **LESS-inspired**, not a reproduction.

Implementation details matter: [the released collector](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/LESS/blob/8abf9628b9a814ac3045445eebc8ba3c908fdc78/less/data_selection/collect_grad_reps.py) normalizes each projected feature. Its candidate Adam proxy uses `(0.9*m+0.1*g)/sqrt(0.999*v+0.001*g*g+1e-8)`, without modifying the saved moments. [The matching code](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/LESS/blob/8abf9628b9a814ac3045445eebc8ba3c908fdc78/less/data_selection/matching.py) averages per-example cosines within a target group, sums checkpoint-weighted group scores, then takes the maximum group. Paper Eq.2 instead describes cosine to a mean target-gradient. We must predeclare which reduction is used; they are not generally equivalent.

[TRAK's primary code](https://github.com/MadryLab/trak) supplies the batched, memory-efficient Rademacher projection used by LESS; a full dense projection matrix must not be allocated. Separately, [Large-Scale Data Selection for Instruction Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01807) reports that complex selectors can underperform random as pools scale. Method complexity and corpus count are not success metrics.

## Proposed frozen protocol

1. **Separate the roles.** Keep the existing 64-question monitoring set labeled adaptive development. Freeze 24 additional official-training-split reference examples as selection-development targets, ideally four each for six named reasoning groups. Exclude their IDs and normalized question hashes from every training pool, warmup and control. Exclude all prior evaluation/probe IDs. Targets may influence selection; they are not independent evidence of success. Official test questions, answers, gradients and even loss values stay sealed until the final model/protocol choice is frozen. No final test data is needed for this design or utility.
2. **Validity first.** Keep the original477 cohort/four arms unchanged. The implementation prepares a new shared cohort only after the new `math_source_teacher1800_v1` teacher pool completes. Hold out human-reference targets, then freeze up to1800 verified candidates for the new gradient/random/skill controls. Preserve exact traces, eligibility decisions and tokenizer hashes; source imports are not automatically valid training examples.
3. **Warm up a selector, not the final student.** Initialize the pinned Granite 4.2 3B with the same r=8, alpha=16 q/v LoRA layout used for final training. Choose 24 candidates uniformly with a fixed independent seed. A proposed pilot permits at most 8192 supervised warmup tokens/eight 1024-token updates, with repetition explicitly recorded if needed, saving after updates four and eight. This is our short warmup choice, not LESS's full recipe. Save optimizer moments and actual mean LR for both intervals. Final comparison arms still initialize independently from the untouched pinned base.
4. **Collect features.** At each warmup checkpoint, run one correctly masked teacher-forced forward/backward pass per candidate and target. Loss is the mean over supervised answer tokens; user/padding labels are -100, no trace is truncated, gradients are cleared between examples, dropout is disabled, and the identical named parameter order/layout hash is used everywhere. Construct candidate directions from the fixed warmup Adam moments; use raw SGD gradients for targets. Project both with one shared seeded Rademacher operator, initially dimension 1024. Store FP32 projected vectors, norms, IDs, question hashes, checkpoint/tokenizer/layout hashes and projection version. Zero/nonfinite vectors fail explicitly. No full-model gradient archive is necessary.
5. **Score and select.** Use the released-code-compatible reduction as the first predeclared variant:

   `S_i,g = sum_c alpha_c * mean_{t in target_group_g} cosine(R*Gamma_i,c, R*grad_t,c)`

   `S_i = max_g S_i,g`, with `alpha_c = mean_LR_c / sum(mean_LR)`.

   Keep signed scores, including negative alignment; stable ties use example ID. Rank by this score alone for the first gradient arm, so any benefit is not confounded with an extra diversity/length heuristic. Reuse the existing exact-token exposure masks and common hash shuffle to allocate 32,768 supervised tokens. Report partial supervised tails and actual example overlap. Additional diversity, score-per-token or group quotas require separately named ablations.
6. **Compare fairly.** Compare gradient, uniform random and current skill-diversity selection on the same pool, 32,768 supervised tokens, 1024 tokens/update, optimizer settings and seed. Keep the current frozen training recipe; any future LR warmup, replay, clipping or weight-decay change must apply to every arm in a new cohort. State that the feature warmup is additional selector cost. Repeat a second training seed if budget permits. Report paired correct/wrong changes, truncation, supervised tokens, total selector-plus-training cost and uncertainty. A loss-alignment score predicting lower target loss need not predict more correct final answers.

## Feasibility and bounded execution

The pinned configuration has hidden size 2560, 40 layers, 40 query heads and eight KV heads. For q/v LoRA rank 8 this implies approximately `40 * 8 * ((2560+2560)+(2560+512)) = 2,621,440` trainable parameters; a worker must confirm the actual named modules/count before collection. One FP32 gradient is about 10 MiB; two Adam moments add about 20 MiB. Frozen base weights and activations dominate GPU memory.

For 477 candidates, 24 targets and two checkpoints, projected FP32 storage is about **3.91 MiB at dimension 1024**, or **31.31 MiB at 8192**. At 10,000 candidates it is about 78.3 MiB/626.5 MiB, respectively. Storage is manageable; backward passes dominate cost. Feature collection scales as two times the number of examples, not as the number of selected training examples.

Before committing the full collection, calibrate 16 length-stratified examples on the same reserved single GPU, including projection and artifact commits. There are approximately 1002 feature backward passes plus warmup in this pilot. A planning example of 0.5 seconds/pass is about 501 seconds; 1.5 seconds/pass is about 1503 seconds before loading/warmup. These are scenarios, not measured throughput. Use an 1800-second hard limit, stop new examples by 1550 seconds, persist partial feature blocks, and refuse a projected overrun. If calibration fails, reduce checkpoints or pool in a new declared pilot; do not silently score only the easiest completed subset.

At the current published A100-80GB+4CPU+32GiB rate used in the project, 1800 seconds is approximately $1.48 compute before startup/storage. A **proposed** $3 collection reservation plus up to $3 per matched training arm must fit the reconciled remaining Modal balance, with final evaluation and serving retained. These are planning envelopes, not reservations or proof that $27 is free to spend. For much larger pools, apply the measured seconds/token calibration before considering another GPU stage. Include selection overhead in the cost comparison rather than amortizing it across hypothetical future runs.

LoRA's initial zero factor makes un-warmed gradients a particularly incomplete representation. Also, the published LESS Adam proxy omits PyTorch AdamW bias correction, decoupled weight decay and clipping; it is an approximation to direction, not an exact optimizer intervention. Pin and disclose these differences rather than adding an unreviewed optimizer change to the running study. A four-checkpoint or 8192-dimension upgrade is only justified by measured ranking stability/accuracy and remaining budget.

## Implemented unpaid utility and remaining work

`granite_query/cloud/gradient_valuation.py` implements finite-vector validation, the read-only LESS Adam proxy, a bounded small Rademacher reference projector, both explicitly named target reductions, deterministic weighted scoring, disjoint training/target identity checks and immutable score exports. Its seven tests cover aligned/opposed directions, checkpoint/group aggregation order, differing reduction policies, projection linearity, moment immutability, immutable source-hashed exports, and rejection of test/overlapping/zero/nonfinite features. It imports no Torch and collects no real model gradients.

Future feature payload: top-level `official_test_used:false`, `target_role:selection_development`, `projection:{family, dimension, seed, parameter_layout_sha256}`, and one to four `checkpoints`. Each checkpoint contains `id`, positive LR `weight`, `candidate_gradient_type:less_adam`, `target_gradient_type:sgd`, and `candidates`/`targets`. Each record carries `id`, `source_split:train`, `question_sha256` and `feature`; candidates additionally require `verified_final_answer:true`, targets a frozen `group`. The scoring utility accepts explicitly named `rademacher_jl` or `countsketch_shake256_v1` features without conflating them.

## Collector implementation update

`cloud/modal_gradient_features.py`, `gradient_feature_control.py` and `gradient_feature_contract.py` now implement the separate bounded stage. They have not been launched. Preparation refuses a growing teacher pool, reserves24 independently referenced human-data targets, freezes a new candidate cohort, and never edits existing study inputs. It saves two selector warmup adapters/optimizer snapshots, length-stratified calibration, per-record features, exact identity/hash manifests and aggregate-only public status. Partial stores cannot be exported as complete. A separate reviewed-protocol marker, fresh saved$3 reservation, and zero other active Modal tasks are required before the launch entrypoint will spawn.

The implemented projection is **CountSketch**, with one signed bucket per parameter from a deterministic SHAKE256 stream, FP32 accumulation on CPU, and O(P+d) temporary storage. This avoids allocating a dense P-by-d matrix and avoids CUDA scatter-add nondeterminism. It is a budget adaptation, **not** the LESS paper's dense Rademacher JL estimator; collisions change variance and may change ranks. Default dimension is1024, with512/2048 permitted as separately frozen settings. The dependency-free reference and vectorized worker mapping are checked against each other. GPU gradient numerics can still vary across hardware/toolchains.

The worker uses a1,800-second timeout with one A10080GB, hard4CPU/32GiB host caps, no automatic retries, a1,550-second new-record guard and a1,650-second export guard. Calibration can refuse the full workload before collection proceeds; that refusal requires a smaller new declared cohort rather than blind retry. A stopped call can resume only after terminal-call reconciliation, preserving input/protocol hashes and ready snapshot hashes. Final controlled student training remains a separate stage starting from fresh base weights. No projected gradients, new selections or accuracy gains should appear on the public results chart until their corresponding artifacts actually exist.
