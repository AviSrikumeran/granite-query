## Question and result

Can a simpler original sculpture communicate selection and extraction while preserving material depth, luminous magenta and pointer response?

The two angular pieces meet along a notched .08-unit joint around a compact core. They counter-slide as rigid groups. The core clears the rear key, then lifts slightly toward the viewer. It returns to its seat before the two pieces close. Smooth quintic easing, a restrained highlight pulse and a small separation settle connect the phases. There are exactly three independently moving groups; touching volumes describe the bracket profiles.

The canvas is up to 840 by 560 CSS pixels in the normal desktop layout, with a taller layout on large screens. It uses a single dominant sculpture rather than splitting the available width between comparisons. Compact controls sit below the artwork. The accepted glyph atlas, face lighting, ambient occlusion, startup reveal, six-level bloom and magenta material are copied into the isolated folder. The mineral shell has a subdued magenta tint and the core holds the strongest brightness. No new external references, assets, services or paid tools were needed. The geometry is original and contains no IBM logo.

## Verification

All new module syntax checks and six automated tests pass. Tests cover 401 rigid-group samples, 801 samples of cross-piece clearance, selection/extraction/return ordering, loop continuity, every part's corners under both pointer extremes across five view sizes, and equality of the established magenta palette and lighting profile. Clearance checks caught an initial key crossing and a lift starting before rear-key clearance; the final paths keep those volumes separate.

Browser verification covers the three poses, range scrubbing to the final joined state, automatic progression through the reveal, pause/replay, keyboard turn/reset, pointer movement at both edges, and WebGL and Canvas 2D rendering. Pointer reads reached about -.099 and +.096 radians on opposite edges. The narrow layout has equal 390-pixel document and scroll widths, with a 350 by 400 artwork view and usable wrapped controls. No warning or error was observed in either renderer.

Reduced-motion startup and preference-change handling, hidden/offscreen suspension and context recovery are source-reviewed. OS reduced motion and GPU context loss were not simulated. Reduced motion holds the revealed pose initially and requires Play to start automatic motion; keyboard inspection remains available. This is a browser prototype, without a field performance or usability result.

## Boundaries and next feedback

This remains a choreographed selection metaphor, not a physics simulation or live training activity. The core is visible while seated and is most prominent at the revealed pose. The material remains glyph-based, and the fallback intentionally approximates its bloom and intermediate shell tint. The paired bracket silhouette and brighter core should be judged together before adding detail. It is not a final logo or an approved full-site palette.

The next useful feedback is whether the resting silhouette reads as two connected pieces, whether the core feels sufficiently distinct, and whether the simpler reveal communicates the intended selection. Keep proof branches in a separate evidence specimen. Do not add the claw back into this view without explicit direction.

Publication is limited to the new specimen, its test and review, the experiment catalogue and design notes. Every existing blob outside that whitelist is checked against the fresh remote tree before the branch update, including homepage and research outputs.
