## Visual question

Can a much more expressive spatial object retain the evidence relationships and direct inspection that made the branch study useful?

The sequence has four distinct moments. Archive packs the surfaces into two narrow banks around a modular core. Scatter bursts them into an irregular suspended field. Trace brings in luminous cubic filaments and moving travel marks. Resolve snaps the surfaces into three source groups and five evidence tiles converging on the finding. The loop closes back to the archive.

The tiles have independently oriented surfaces, spines and raised details, using the selected magenta material rather than a new palette. Thirty-two active volumes share the existing 40-slot renderer. Curves are projected from world coordinates and masked by the solid volumes' projected hulls so they pass behind the records. The eight tiles represent three source routes and five evidence requirements from one study, not eight independent studies.

Dragging changes one record's position and its attached curves. Release uses a damped spring; nearby surfaces respond slightly to pointer proximity. Selecting a record highlights its ancestry and related evidence while the rest of the sculpture stays visible. The complete context returns through the core or scene controls. Full canvas provides a larger viewing area without browser fullscreen permissions.

The inherited renderer's narrow vignette initially hid a peripheral tile. Widening that envelope and adjusting one tile's spacing made all eight surfaces visible. Geometry checks also caught pulled records reaching the view edge; revised framing keeps the inspected extremes inside the canvas.
