## Question and result

Can the existing proof branches become a claw that grips and pulls the selected magenta core out of its shell?

The same ten-joint, nine-edge graph reshapes into three jaws with five contact pads. The shell clears first, the open fingers descend, and the pads close against the core. Only then do claw and core rise together. The links rotate with their endpoints, and the existing material shading follows those orientations. The claw is less luminous than the core to preserve their visual separation.

The implementation reuses existing repository specimens and the previously captured design evidence. No new external references, assets or paid services were needed. The branch topology and initial node positions are retained, while its original orthogonal links are replaced by straight articulated links. The housing and core are copied from the earlier extraction into an independent specimen.

## Verification

Eight automated checks pass. They cover exact sampling of the prior extraction, source branch placement, 161 samples of oriented-link connectivity, ordered opening/reach/grip/lift, stable five-pad contact through the pull, 201 collision samples against the shell and core, framing at pointer extremes on narrow and wide canvases, and loop continuity. A contact check caught one link corner entering the core; slightly wider pads and a matching contact radius resolved it.

Browser checks cover the five stage controls, timeline scrubbing, automatic playback, pause/replay, linked mouse response, linked and independent keyboard inspection, and both oriented rendering paths. The narrow layout has equal 375-pixel document and scroll widths. No warning or error was observed. Reduced-motion, offscreen suspension and hidden-page policies are source-reviewed; OS reduced motion and GPU context loss were not simulated. No field performance claim is made.

## Remaining design questions

Some finger contacts are naturally occluded by the housing in the neutral view; mouse rotation helps inspect them. The angular claw remains an abstract mechanism, and its links change length during articulation. There is no force simulation, release/drop, final logo, stone fracture or evidence-network expansion in this pass. The next visual refinement should respond to a specific silhouette or timing observation, keeping this version as a baseline.

Publishing is limited to the new specimen, its test and review, workflow notes and experiment catalogue. All earlier study folders, the homepage, training code and research outputs are preserved.
