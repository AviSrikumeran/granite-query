02 / The record

Protocol and comparison

Conditions come before interpretation.

A protocol identifies which questions were considered, how training exposure was bounded and which model revision was used. These fields define the scope of a comparison.

Question cohort
Defines the questions the comparison actually covers.
Model revision
Identifies the starting model rather than relying on a changing name.
Training exposure
States the budget and what was matched between conditions.
Evaluation scope
Separates development observations from broader claims.

Matched exposure should be explicit. A missing record stays missing; the interface should not fill it with a plausible value.

03 / The record

The qualified finding

State a result at the strength its evidence supports.

A completed job is not automatically an improvement. A result needs a named comparison, an evaluation scope and its uncertainty. Negative and inconclusive findings belong beside positive ones.

Authoring principle / not a measured result

A finding should travel with its comparison, sources and limitations.

  • Name what is being compared.
  • Keep the scope and uncertainty beside the claim.
  • Let readers inspect the underlying records.

The example here is a reporting principle. It is not an experiment outcome and does not imply that Granite Query improved.

05 / Reading responsibly

Limitations stay visible

Make the boundary as discoverable as the finding.

A single development study cannot establish broad generalization. Uncertainty, missing records and changes to the method must remain visible before a reader turns a finding into a broader claim.

Limits of this demonstration

  • All content in this note is authored for the interface study.
  • No model was run, no accuracy is reported and no improvement is claimed.
  • The search is local keyword matching across six sections.
  • Navigation behavior is demonstrated; usability has not been measured.

A document is clearer when its limitations are ordinary navigation destinations, rather than a footnote a reader has to hunt for.
